Where they are now

Every real function ww computes is in one place, src/fmath.cpp (ww's own, and since 0.9.5076 glibc's exp, expm1, log1p, sinh, cosh and tanh); nothing in ww's C++ calls the system's math library. Over it are names: Math for ww programs, Complex, the whole-list functions, Formula's spreadsheet functions, and Snippy's math and cmath, which is now only Complex. What is more than one is what only Snippy computes (lib/Snippy/Runtime/ModMath.ww), which no ww program can reach:

  • Floats: gamma and lgamma (CPython's Lanczos sums), erf and erfc (worked out to more bits and rounded once), cbrt, fma, remainder, copysign, nextafter, ulp, degrees and radians, hypot of any number of coordinates and dist (CPython's vector_norm), fsum and sumprod.
  • Integers: factorial, comb and perm (products of ranges), lcm.
  • Two hypots, three ways: Math.hypot(x, y) in C++ and Snippy's __pyNorm in WinterWolf are the same rule (the nearest float, as CPython's math.hypot) written twice; Complex's __cmHypot (0.9.5076) is glibc's, as CPython's cmath and abs() of a complex call, and stays: it is a different answer on purpose.
  • cbrt is glibc's code. pyCbrt (0.9.5059) is glibc's sysdeps/ieee754/dbl-64/s_cbrt.c: its polynomial's coefficients, its table of 2^(±1/3) and its Halley step. That file is under the LGPL, which ww's own code may not take (glibc's IBM sin, cos, tan and atan2 are left alone for the same reason). It goes.

isqrt and gcd are already ww's (BigInt.sqrt(), BigInt.gcd), and fmod is ww's float %.

What does not change

  • Snippy's answers, but for cbrt: each function moves as it is, checked by tests/snippy_programs/lib/mod_math.py and mod_fractions_statistics.py (statistics uses erfc).
  • Math.hypot(x, y), and Complex's glibc hypot.
  • The same bits on every system: the float functions in src/fmath.cpp beside the rest (question 2), with no multiply and add fused but where written as std::fma.

Each new function gets a test of its own (tests/math_more.ww), its values from CPython where CPython's are the same rule, and docs/language.html describes each.

Questions for the user

Each with the recommendation and why.

2Where the float functions are computed

  • In C++, in src/fmath.cpp beside sin and exp, with its double-double arithmetic for the extra bits erf and gamma need (Snippy's do it with BigInts now).
  • In WinterWolf, moved from Snippy nearly as they are. Math is an external object, which cannot hold a function with a WinterWolf body (A2 met this: frexp became a float's method), so either the compiler learns to let it, or these become floats' methods (x.gamma()) and list functions.

Recommended: C++. It is where every other real function is, it is many times faster than BigInt arithmetic, and it keeps Math one object. The ww-first rule asks for WinterWolf unless C++ is needed; here Math's being external is what needs it, short of a compiler change.

Decided2026-11-23, the user ("go with Recommended for all 4"): as recommended.

3cbrt

  • ww's own: the nearest float, exact for exact cubes (cbrt(27) is 3.0). CPython on Linux gives glibc's, which is not always the nearest (3.0000000000000004), so Snippy's math.cbrt then differs from CPython in the last digit now and then; mod_math.py's line of cbrt values changes to ones where they agree, and the difference is written down.
  • No cbrt: glibc's taken out and nothing put in its place.

Recommended: ww's own. glibc's bits cannot be had without glibc's code; the nearest float is the better answer, and it is what Java's and Kotlin's cbrt aim for.

Decided2026-11-23, the user ("go with Recommended for all 4"): as recommended.

4fsum's and sumprod's names

  • exactSum() and exactDot(other) on lists of floats, beside sum() and dot(), which add as they go.
  • Math.fsum(values) and Math.sumprod(p, q), Python's names.
  • Make sum() and dot() exact for floats: one name each, but slower, and the answers of existing programs change.

Recommended: exactSum() and exactDot(). Neither Kotlin nor Java has one to copy; these say what they do and sit beside the plain ones.

Decided2026-11-23, the user ("go with Recommended for all 4"): as recommended.

Built (0.9.5078)

Every name in the table, as decided. In src/fmath.cpp: cbrt (a guess from pow, a Newton step, then the cube's shortfall in two doubles as one correction: the nearest float on all 44,796 inputs checked exactly with fractions, every exact cube's root exact), gamma and lnGamma (CPython's Lanczos steps, the same as Snippy's were), erf and erfc (a series with no cancelling terms, and a continued fraction from 4 up, both in two doubles; e^z worked out to 2^-100 for them, as fmath's own e^z kernel, good to 2^-64, is not enough where erfc is 1 - erf), fma, ieeeRemainder (CPython's m_remainder, from fmod), and the length of a vector of any size (squares in two doubles). In WinterWolf: the floats' withSign, nextUp, nextDown, nextTowards and ulp (builtins.ww), exactSum and exactDot (numeric.ww), BigInt's factorial, binomial, permutations and lcm (bigint.ww). Snippy's math is now Python's names and checks over these: lib/Snippy/Runtime/ModMath.ww went from 870 lines to 463, and glibc's cbrt is gone.

Checked against what Snippy gave before, on 247,000 inputs: gamma, lnGamma, hypot of three, fma and remainder the same bits everywhere; erf and erfc the same everywhere but below about 2^-147, where Snippy's fixed point of 200 bits lost the digits of erf (giving 0 below about 2^-200): the new answer is the nearer one in all 4,325 such cases, against a 140-digit series. Against CPython 3.14 on Linux: erf, erfc, fma, remainder and hypot of three the same on every input; gamma (42 of 34,514) and lgamma (5 of 30,000) differ in the last digit, through ww's own exp, pow and sin under them, as they did before; cbrt is the nearest float where glibc's is not (Math.cbrt(27) is 3.0, CPython's 3.0000000000000004), so mod_math.py's line takes cbrt(1000) instead. Tests: tests/math_more.ww (numbers from tools/fmath/python_more.py), and every Snippy program as CPython's.
